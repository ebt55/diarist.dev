Choosing a runtime package and models

Diarist runs one speech runtime with a speaker model and one of several speech models, and since 0.1.0-beta.5 a punctuation model on the CPU with its own runtime; all are files in your data folder. This page is for picking them by what your PC has, and for seeing the numbers behind the defaults. Published numbers come from leaderboards and say so; measured numbers were taken on {{machine}} and say when. The charts are drawn from docs/eval/benchmarks.json in the repository, which names the file each number comes from.

CUDA package: the NVIDIA driver has to be new enough for CUDA 13. R580 or newer is the estimate; 610.88 is measured working.

CUDA kernels are built for Turing (sm_75), Ampere (sm_86), Ada (sm_89) and Blackwell (sm_120). An NVIDIA card older than Turing uses the Vulkan package.

Vulkan package: measured on the RTX 3080 only, 16 Oct 2026, through the app's own path on a 5-minute clip of the AMI meeting: 25 to 28 times real time (CUDA 67 to 69 on the same clip), the same word count as CUDA with 3.2 percent of the words different, and the same speakers. Nothing is known about AMD or Intel GPUs yet.

The setup screen checks the GPU (NVML for NVIDIA, DXGI for any vendor), preselects the package and lets you change it. Package sizes: CUDA 98 MB, Vulkan 19 MB, CPU 6 MB. The app reads free GPU memory before it starts a job.

Punctuation is the same on every PC: the punctuation model (Horizon-Labs punctuation-restoration-small, 306 MB with its tokenizer, ONNX Runtime 1.31.0 and licence files) runs on the CPU after the speech pass, whatever the package, and uses no GPU memory.

Speech to text, published

Speaker assignment needs the start and end of every word, and live recording needs a streaming model. The two models at the top of the leaderboard give no word timestamps and run only inside a Python stack, so Diarist cannot use them. Nemotron 3.5 has no leaderboard number yet; the measured comparison below is the basis for choosing between it and the EN model.

Measured in Diarist

The evaluation harness in the repository runs the app's own command-line tool over the author's meetings and one public AMI meeting, and scores the result against a reference. Everything in this section was measured on the development PC, with an RTX 3080 10 GB, and each part says when. Imports and the pass after Stop run with the file settings 0.1.0-beta.5 ships: the speech model at its full lookahead (1.12 s) and the diarizer with its offline geometry. The chart is the 0.1.0-beta.5 release check, {{measured_long}}, which also collapses repeated-word bursts and adds punctuation; punctuation never changes a word, so the scores are the same without it.

{{chart_measured}}

Over the whole evaluation set, 36 recordings and 28 hours, cpWER with the EN model went from 11.01 percent with 0.1.0-beta.4 to 10.85, all of it from the collapsed bursts, and stayed at 11.30 with Nemotron 3.5; no recording got worse.

Speaker attribution was close with both models: 0.2 to 0.3 percent of words went to the wrong speaker on the four Zoom meetings, 2.2 to 2.3 on the far-field AMI meeting; within a second of a speaker change, 5.5 to 7.8 percent with Nemotron 3.5 and 5.6 to 12.7 with the EN model (16 Oct 2026). So the choice comes down to the words: the EN model keeps more of what was said; Nemotron 3.5 drops about 40 percent of filler words, keeps quiet speech the EN model drops, and covers 40+ languages with automatic detection.

Peak GPU memory was measured on 15 Oct 2026. The 560 ms live lookahead of 0.1.0-beta.5 does not change it: measured on 27 Oct 2026, the live server with both sessions stayed within 3 MiB per session of the runtime's default lookahead.

Speed

  • Import: about {{import_factor}} times real time on the RTX 3080 over the whole import, so an hour of audio takes about two minutes. Measured over the seven evaluation sessions, 324 minutes of audio, 16 Oct 2026; the speech pass alone runs 76 to 78 times real time, the rest is a standalone diarization pass the app skips.
  • Punctuation, after the speech pass: about 5 to 6 seconds of CPU per hour of audio (5.3 to 5.6 over the 28 hours of the evaluation set), 0.65 GB of RAM while it runs, no GPU memory. Measured on the development PC, 27 Oct 2026.
  • Live: the first words of an utterance appear about {{live_first_text}} s after they are spoken, and two channels (microphone and system audio) keep up in real time. Since 0.1.0-beta.5 live recording runs with 560 ms of lookahead, which makes fewer errors than the runtime's 160 ms, where words appeared after about 0.14 s. Measured on an RTX 3080 10 GB, 27 Oct 2026.

Languages, measured

The EN model transcribes English only. Nemotron 3.5 covers 40+ languages, and the app's language lists offer the 30 its model card marks transcription-ready or broad-coverage; a recording or import in a language the selected model does not cover runs on an installed model that does. Eight of those languages were run through Diarist's own runtime on about ten minutes of public FLEURS audio each, with the language given and with automatic detection, next to the model card's own FLEURS number. These are smoke tests on small slices, not Diarist's published accuracy: read them for direction.

{{chart_languages}}

Set the meeting's language when you know it: automatic detection loses about a second of the first utterance and costs 0.1 to 3 points against a given language on the model card, most for Hindi, Russian, Ukrainian, Polish, Bulgarian, Croatian and Romanian. The eight languages the card lists as adaptation-ready (Greek, Lithuanian, Latvian, Maltese, Slovenian, Hebrew, Thai, Norwegian Nynorsk) are not offered, because the model needs fine-tuning for them; any tag can still be typed under "Other tag", with a warning. The full table is in section 6 of the model guide.

Caveats

  • Parakeet drops speech. Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 scores about 0.6 WER points lower than the EN model and writes real punctuation, but it silently dropped a 46-second span and two 10-second spans from a 77-minute file, and a 2-minute cut around the gap lost the same span. It is experimental and off by default.
  • Quiet speech with the EN model. On about 10 minutes of FLEURS English, where 36 of the 55 recordings peak below -30 dBFS, the EN model scored 21.5 percent WER, nearly all of it words left out, against 8.8 for Nemotron 3.5. For a soft-spoken participant, a far microphone or a file recorded at a low level, use Nemotron 3.5 or a louder source.
  • Eight speakers. The diarizer has eight labels per recording. A ninth person is merged into an existing label; split a recording that holds several meetings before importing it.
  • Speakers with a few words. Imports and the pass after Stop can merge a participant who says only a sentence or two into another label: on one Zoom meeting a participant with 7 words lost their label with both speech models (5 speakers found as 4). It is the cost of the offline diarizer geometry, which elsewhere puts fewer words near a speaker change on the wrong speaker.
  • Punctuation. The Nemotron models punctuate little (0.2 and 2.5 percent of words), so since 0.1.0-beta.5 a punctuation model adds periods, commas, question marks and sentence capitals after Stop and on imports, without changing a word. It is a second model's guess: against Zoom's own transcripts of the author's meetings, about two in three of its periods and question marks are where Zoom put one, its question marks are right 0.39 of the time on the far-field AMI meeting, and it writes about one comma per sentence where Zoom writes about two. Hindi, Japanese and Chinese get no commas, Thai is not punctuated, and the live view stays unpunctuated.
  • Overlap and far-field audio. People talking over each other, far-field microphones and noisy rooms degrade both the words and the labels; the AMI row above shows how much.
  • Vulkan and CPU. Both packages run since 0.1.0-beta.2 and were measured on the development PC only: Vulkan on the RTX 3080, CPU on a Ryzen 9 5900X. AMD and Intel GPUs are untested.
  • What to expect. On clean meeting audio about one word in 15 to 20 is wrong or missing with any of these models; on far-field room recordings about one in 9.

Known issues lists each of these with its status.
